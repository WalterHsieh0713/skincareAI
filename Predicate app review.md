# Predicate App Reviews

Notes on existing apps in or adjacent to Dewpoint's space — what they do well, where they fall short, and what's worth borrowing or avoiding.

---

## Yuka

**Pros**
- Product scanning is very accurate, with a detailed and complete ingredient list. Scanning is quick and efficient, and there's no need to switch scanners between food and beauty products.
- A leaderboard feature ranks products within each category (sunscreens, toothpastes, conditioners, facial cleansers, yogurts, snacks, candies, etc.). The full leaderboard unlocks by inviting a friend.
- A recommendations feature surfaces a list of scanned food or beauty products and suggests healthier alternatives — not necessarily identical products, but similar ones.
- Each ingredient carries a risk rating, and the overall product score is calculated from its ingredients. Users can drill into any ingredient to see what specific risks it's associated with.
- A pay-what-you-want subscription (roughly $10/$15/$20 per year, user's choice) unlocks the product search feature.
- The app is explicit about not being sponsored by any brands, which reinforces trust in its ratings and scores being unbiased.

**Cons**
- Ratings feel overly strict — most scanned food items and products came back as "poor" or "bad." The recommendations list only sorts by popularity and score; a price sort would help, since healthier (manufactured) alternatives tend to run pricier.
- The scanning UI is poor — the interface fades out during scanning in a way that's confusing for first-time users trying to exit.
- The overall interface could be more polished. It currently feels basic/default rather than visually attractive.
- No widget support — adding one would let users launch the scanner faster from the lock screen.

## Neutrogena Skin360 & L'Oréal Skin Genius

**Pros**
- Both have a delicate, polished UI — the wording and sample imagery feel very professional, which tracks given they're backed by established cosmetic/skincare brands.
- L'Oréal generates an entire list of recommended products based on your skincare profile scan results.
- L'Oréal breaks your skin profile down into distinct major categories and scores each one individually (e.g., acne, wrinkles, eye bags, etc.).
- Neutrogena's scanning UI is excellent — interactive and highly responsive throughout the scan itself.
- Neutrogena asks follow-up questions during the flow to gather more information, which makes the resulting skin analysis more accurate.
- There's a lot to learn from both here — we should study their scanning and results flows to make Dewpoint's scan easier to use while staying accurate.

**Cons**
- Both apps are essentially single-feature — they only offer the skincare scanner and don't extend beyond it, aside from recommending their own product lines.
- Neither has a routine adherence, ingredient conflict, or progress-tracking layer, which is exactly the gap Dewpoint can exploit by being a well-rounded skincare tracking app instead of a scan-then-upsell tool.
